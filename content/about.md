  heading: "How we got here"
  parts:
    - body: |
        Euro Team Outreach began in 2004, founded by three friends — Joshua Steele, Nathan Day, and Jessie Beal — who shared a conviction that Ukraine was worth a life's work. (Joshua had already made Lviv his home in 2001, and has now served in Ukraine for twenty-five years.)

        For most of the next two decades, the heart of ETO was **Carpathian Mountain Outreach (CMO)** — a summer missions project in the Carpathian mountains that, over seventeen years and thirteen projects, brought more than sixty young people to Ukraine to learn the work of a missionary by doing it: preaching at film showings, distributing literature, and sharing daily life with our staff. Out of that same season came **Bible First**, the study course that became our primary way of teaching the Scriptures, and our Ukrainian translation and distribution of the ***Good and Evil*** book.

    - body: |
        Then the war changed the landscape. When russia's full-scale invasion came in 2022, our family was displaced for a season, and the work bent toward the immediate needs of a country under fire — refugee ministry, and tens of thousands of *Good and Evil* books carried into war-affected regions. We returned to Lviv in 2023, and the final CMO project ran that summer.

        What rose in its place is **Ukraine Gospel Outreach (UGO)**. In 2024 we partnered with Ukrainian missionary Oleksandr Ilchenko to carry the Gospel — and practical aid — into the newly de-occupied villages of the south, places the larger aid groups had mostly left behind. UGO is our flagship today: the same message, carried by changed methods, into some of the hardest-hit corners of Ukraine.

Euro Team Outreach is a Christian missions organization that has served in Ukraine since 2004 — a small team of American missionary families, rooted in the city of Lviv and working hand in hand with Ukrainian believers and local churches.

We exist for one reason: to make Jesus Christ known. For more than twenty years, through changing seasons and now through a war, that single calling has held steady — even as the way we live it out has changed again and again.
